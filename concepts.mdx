## The pieces

**Zend identity.** One account per person across Zend and every ZApp. Every app gets the user's `appUserId`, username, name and avatar. Email, wallet address and contacts need the user's consent.

**ZApp.** Your web app, hosted on your own HTTPS origins and registered with a [manifest](/guides/manifest).

**Bridge.** `@zendfi/appkit` talks to Zend with JSON messages. In the mobile app it uses a WebView bridge; on the web it uses `postMessage` to the `app.usezend.app` frame. Zend checks that every message comes from one of your registered origins.

**Interaction.** A stateful object with an ID: a challenge, a round, a booking. Your manifest defines its states and allowed moves; Zend stores it and enforces them. See [Interactions](/guides/interactions).

**Card.** A chat or feed item that Zend draws from a template you fill in. Cards show the live state of their interaction. See [Cards](/guides/cards).

## Who owns what

| Zend | Your ZApp |
|---|---|
| Sign-in, consent, profile | Your logic and data |
| Every money screen, limits, payment records | Text, emoji, image and buttons on cards |
| Interaction storage and allowed state changes | The state machine itself, in your manifest |
| Drawing cards and bubbles, posting rules | When to post |
| Webhook delivery | Handling webhooks |

Rule of thumb: Zend enforces anything that involves money, identity, or what other people see.

## Lifecycle of a chat ZApp

<Steps>
  <Step title="The user starts it">
    Tunde taps **+ → Apps → Challenge** in a chat with Danny.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Zend creates the interaction">
    Zend creates a `challenge` interaction with both people and opens your `sheet` path with `?interaction=int_...`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Consent, once">
    The first time, Zend shows "Continue to Accident as Tunde?".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your page runs">
    Your page reads `zend.launch.interaction`, runs the game, and calls `zend.interaction.update(...)` and `zend.postCard(...)`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The other person joins">
    Danny sees a card in the chat. Its button opens your app on the same interaction.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your server follows along">
    Your server receives `interaction.updated` webhooks and can make moves only it is allowed to make.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Private and public apps

|  | Private (default) | Public |
|---|---|---|
| Review | None | Automated checks, then Zend approval |
| Label | "Made by {you}. Not checked by Zend." | None |
| Listed on Discover | No | Yes |
| Limits | `private` tier | `public` tier |
| Custody | `contract` or `creator` | `contract` only |
| `public` interaction visibility | Not allowed | Allowed |

Anyone with the link `app.usezend.app/apps/<slug>` can open a private app. See [Public apps](/ship/public-apps).

## Custody

Custody decides where money from groups, events and prizes sits before it's paid out. Set it with `custody` in the manifest.

| Value | Where the money sits | What users see |
|---|---|---|
| `contract` (default) | In Zend's on-chain contract until its rules decide who gets it | "Held safely until the rules say who gets it" |
| `creator` | Goes straight to the person who made the group or event | "Goes straight to {name}. Zend can't get it back." |

## Trust boundaries

<Warning>
Your page is untrusted from Zend's point of view, and from yours. Anyone can edit it in their browser.
</Warning>

- Your page can propose money actions. Only the user can approve them, in Zend's sheet.
- A resolved promise in the browser is not proof of payment. Confirm with the `payment.completed` webhook or the server API. See [Verifying payments](/money/verifying-payments).
- `appSession` tokens last 10 minutes. Exchange them on your server and start your own session; don't use them as your session.
- Moves that decide outcomes, such as declaring a winner, belong on your server. Mark them `serverOnly` in the manifest so a page can't make them.
