Two Zend pieces run on a board today:

- **[Devices](/devices):** the board asks people to pay its owner, shares payments, sells passes, checks people in, takes settings and commands from the owner's phone, alerts them, and sends readings Zend signs. It holds no money and can't send any.
- **[Bots](/bots):** a Raspberry Pi (or any board running Node) runs a bot account. It can message people, post to the feed, and get paid by @handle.

Each idea below is marked with what it needs:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Device** | Works today with the [device API](/devices) |
| **Bot** | Works today with a bot account on the board |
| **Later** | Needs something Zend doesn't have yet (named in the idea) |

## Sensors that settle payments

A device's signed reading can release a [hold](/money/prizes#prize-or-hold) in a ZApp. The payer still has the wait after the result to stop a payout from a tampered device.

| Project | Board and parts | How it works | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Delivery box that pays the rider** | Raspberry Pi, lid switch, distance sensor | The sender holds the fee; the box sends `delivered` when the parcel is in, and the ZApp pays the rider. Full code in `examples/delivery_box.py` and `examples/zapp_settle_hold.mjs` | Device + ZApp |
| **Cold-chain transport** | ESP32, temperature sensor | The cooler sends a signed temperature every 10 minutes; the transporter is paid only if every reading stayed in range | Device + ZApp |
| **Produce by weight** | Raspberry Pi, load cell | The aggregator's scale sends the weight; the farmer is paid for exactly those kilos from a held amount | Device + ZApp |
| **Borehole or solar install bounty** | ESP32, flow or current sensor | The installer is paid when the sensor shows water flowing or panels producing for a day | Device + ZApp |
| **Generator maintenance** | ESP32 on the controller | Run-hours readings prove the generator ran after the technician's visit | Device + ZApp |

## Boards that talk to you

These don't take payments at all. They use a bot account to message people or post to the feed.

| Project | Board and parts | How it works | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Doorbell that messages you** | Raspberry Pi, button, camera | Someone rings; your bot DMs you "Someone's at the gate" | Bot |
| **Generator and fuel reminder** | Raspberry Pi, run-hours sensor | DMs you when the generator has run 200 hours, or when fuel is low | Bot |
| **Fish pond or poultry monitor** | Raspberry Pi, temperature and water sensors | DMs the farmer when the water is too warm or a feeder is empty | Bot |
| **Shop open sign** | Raspberry Pi, door switch | Posts "We're open" to your bot's feed when the shop door opens in the morning | Bot |
| **Borehole or tank level** | Raspberry Pi, ultrasonic sensor | Messages everyone in the compound who wrote to the bot when the tank is nearly empty | Bot |

A bot can message people who wrote to it first, and people who use the ZApp it belongs to. Message your own bot once to start getting alerts.

## Picking a first build

| If you have | Start with |
|---|---|
| An ESP32, a relay and a button | Pay-per-wash or the busker's tip jar |
| An ESP32, a relay and an OLED | The gym door (passes and check-in) |
| A Raspberry Pi and a speaker | The party jukebox |
| A Raspberry Pi and a button | The doorbell bot |
| A Raspberry Pi, a switch and a ZApp | The delivery box that pays the rider |
| A classroom of ESP32s | The vending machine lesson |

Every device build follows the same three steps: `zend device pair`, ask for something (`request()`, `checkin()` or `reading()`), then act on the verified event. See [Devices](/devices).
